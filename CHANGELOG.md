# Changelog

What changed for a player, newest first. The game shows the same text: CHANGELOG on its title screen.

## 0.4: The screen burns (in progress)

- Each warden has a bullet phase at 60% of its health: Gatejaw's rings with a turning gap, Mother Morel's bloom of spores, the Unmaker's walls with doorways. A banner names it, and walking finds the way through.
- Dice now roll in with ordinary waves: from minute 5 on floor 1, sooner further down, and loaded dice from floor 2. A knock back still calls far more of them, and a warden fights without them.
- The house: three new monsters, one on each floor. The dealer fans five cards, the roulette wheel sweeps an arm of balls round, the slot machine shows three symbols and pays out the attack they name. Each comes alone the first time.
- Rooms overload: a room at three stars with two resonating neighbours lets go of a burst in its element every 20 seconds, by itself. Fire burns, frost freezes, water shields you if you stand in it, and so on for all eight. The draft card says OVERLOAD when a pick gets a room there.
- You, enemy bullets and pickups are now drawn above every blast, glow, spark and number, and above the screen's flash.
- An elite's death has a ring, a boom and a shake; a warden's a bigger ring. The screen never flashes more than three times a second.
- Enemy bullets have a dark rim, so they show on every floor and every carpet.
- The daily delve: DAILY on the title starts the same run for everyone for a day: the same floors, the same delver, the same cards behind the hearth's doors. Your first try counts; after that it is practice.
- A share card: the end screen hands on your floor plan as a picture (R) or as text with a coloured square per room (H). It carries the game's name and address and nothing about you.
- Phones: the game now takes two screen pixels for each of its own, so everything is twice as large. Every screen has a layout for a phone held upright and on its side, the draft shows one card at a time upright, and everything you tap is finger-sized.
- The music can follow the floor plan: Music grows in the pause menu. WITH THE PLAN brings a floor's piece in voice by voice as you build. BY ELEMENT adds a voice for each element among your rooms.
- A music player on the title screen: every track, switches for its voices and for the element voices, every sound effect, and the game's creatures dancing to it.
- Going back to the title from a run is no longer deafening. The rooms that the title builds behind its logo used to sound all at once.

## 0.3: The card decides the run (2026-11-21)

- Doors cost more the deeper you are. A sealed door adds to the knocking: 11% on floor 1, 15% on floor 2, 17% on floor 3. The HUD and each card show it.
- Rooms do the work. A room's power is 0.85, 1.6 and 2.6 at one, two and three stars, resonance is +40% per link, and room power keeps pace with the monsters downstairs.
- Each floor's elite goes for the room you built up most and switches it off while it stands there.
- The Stairwell is a sharper choice. Its bar says how strong the warden would be now and what a fast kill pays. Late in the dread clock the knocking grows, and a warden gets faster the longer its fight lasts.
- A damage tally on the end screen and in the pause menu: which weapons, rooms and charms did the run's damage.
- Pocket Bombs are trimmed.
- Stakes: after a first win the hero screen offers a ladder of six steps, one more rule each.
- Each floor has its own music: a piece of 48 to 56 bars on the floor's old tune, darker the deeper you go.
- A tick in the music is gone.
- A changelog: this file, and a screen in the game.
- Every version keeps its own address, so an older one can be played next to the new one.

## 0.2: The first public version (2026-11-20)

- Three floors, 36 room cards, 24 weapons with an evolution each, 27 charms, four delvers and a deck you build between runs.
- A 30-second intro film before the title.
- The first draft teaches the card in the game, and H explains a card again.
- Knock back: a new hand behind a door, paid for in danger.
- Walking with the mouse, and first hints for touch.
